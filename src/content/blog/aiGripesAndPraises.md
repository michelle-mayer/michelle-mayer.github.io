Everyone inside and outside of tech seems to have an opinion about AI right now. I'm lucky to have a front seat to this transformation.

As a software engineer at Microsoft, AI has been thrust into my life like a tidal wave. For better or for worse, it's here. I *mostly* agree with AI advocates that say "the train is coming and we must get on board". If bad actors, competing businesses, foreign adversaries, etc are going to use this new technology to improve, we will be vulnerable if we ignore it.

BUT I'll be the first to admit that I can't predict the future. And with many new inventions, there are many moral shades of grey. There are right ways to go about it and wrong way. There are major risks and tradeoffs. However, the point of this list isn't to write a detailed essay that dissects the complexity and moral ambiguity of this "AI Era". 

I wanted to share my experiences with the positive and negative ways that AI has altered my day-to-day as a Software Engineer. To my fellow devs, I'd love to hear if you relate to any of these or if you have additional perspectives. Lastly, know that you are infinitely more valuable than any AI model-- simply because you're a living, breathing, human ❤️.  

## Gripes

### The "move faster or fall behind" pressure

The message I keep hearing is the same: use AI, ship more, or you will fall behind. Employees who can't get on the train will frankly be let go... I do appreciate the managers who give realistic advice to prevent people from being blindsided with negative performances and job eliminations... Unfortunately, individual devs and lower-level managers have no control over broad company strategy. Even C-suite employees have to answer to the board who demand ROI from the *insane* levels of AI buildout expenditure. However, ==simply demanding an extreme level of ROI doesn't automatically mean these new AI tools enable it.==

It is true that agentic coding has significantly increased the rate at which developers can churn out code (site github article). But, a few questions remain. Firstly, does the increased output directly correlate to improved business outcomes-- meaning the a majority of the increased output is quality, maintainable, profitable, and *necessary* (will discuss more in point 2). Secondly, how is this increased output pressure affecting employees?

The days when a developer was expected to prioritize one or two deliverables at once are over. I usually have at least 5 repositories open in VSCode at all times during the workday. And within them, numerous Github Copilot agents churning away at my prompts, burning through tokens like a wildfire. The productivity is insane. BUT the **constant** context switching is mentally overwhelming. "Hey Michelle can I have an update on this task?" Oh which osdfsdfsdf.....
It can be an act of "bravery" to simply admit as a dev these days that you feel like you're drowning.

Meanwhile, a fair share of the satisfaction from owning, planning, and delivering the feature is gone. With agency, the whole definition of ownership is blurred. Is it "my accomplishment" if the agent fleshed out most of the logic? But it WILL be my neck on the line if it breaks. 

In response to this pressure, employees are discarding anything that is a hindrance to direct productivity. This has led to a death in team and company culture. Asking someone about how their day is going for example... 

Triangle of quality, speed, work life balance

And finally, with all of the back-breaking work developers are doing to keep a company from becoming impotent in this AI era, it is too early for the developers to see any of the profits trickle down. The output you're expecting from devs is say, 5x, but the incentive is a 1% annual merit increase that doesn't even match inflation. OKAY... 

> [!QUOTE]
> Even if companies are solely acting of a "bottom line" mindset, they should care about the mental wellbeing of their employees-- because stressed and burnt-out employees are less productive. [We Can't Automate Our Way Out of Burnout](/blog/humancapitalintech/).

### Fitting a square peg into a round hole
Along with the pressure to deliver faster with AI is the general pressure to integrate it into any feature possible. You see it with many businesses-- AI becoming a marketing buzzword in places where it doesn't add actual value. As a developer, we are continually brainstorming ways to modernize flows for better customer experience and value. However, SOME FEATURES SIMPLY DON'T NEED AI INTEGRATED 😂.
