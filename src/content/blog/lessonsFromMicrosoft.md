I joined Microsoft in 2022 as a software engineer. I've learned plenty of technical
things since then, but the lessons that changed how I work the most weren't in any
onboarding doc. They were about people: how we handle change, how we share the
load, and how we show up for each other.

These are the ones I keep coming back to.

## Your relatives will text you about all things Microsoft
Starting the list off on a humorous note, one of the funniest things about working for a company as big as Microsoft is the feedback from friends and family. Any issue with Outlook, payments, Windows login, etc are routed to me with excitement that they "know someone on the inside". When in reality, I am the size of an ant compared to the ~200k employees needed to maintain an ecosystem as large as Microsoft. I'm flattered that they think I can help. If they have a question about the niche internal app my team develops, I'm the girl! Otherwise, ummm have you tried the 1800 support number? :) It's also funny the shade they throw during outages. Like "did you know Teams is broken for all customers"? Yes in fact, I was aware the world is burning down and thank goodness I'm not the on-call dev for that team.

## Getting into a company like Microsoft is a combination of luck and talent
When I started working at Microsoft, I was surprised the amount of cold pings I got in teams from individuals asking for referrals to certain Microsoft jobs. 

## Change is constant
I am someone who doesn't love change, which is probably a scary thing to admit in tech :)  From reorgs, to reprioritizations, attrition, you name it-- it feels like were always "storming". In my four years at Microsoft, I've had 3 different managers and worked on 5 different products.
