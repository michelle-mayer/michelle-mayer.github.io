## Accountability and shame are two different things

Accountability sounds like, "I made a mistake. Here's what happened, and here's
how I'll fix it." Shame sounds like, "I *am* the mistake."

They can feel alike in the moment, but they pull in opposite directions.
Accountability looks at the problem and what to do next. Shame looks at the
person, and it teaches people to hide their next mistake instead of raising it
early. Brené Brown draws a similar line between guilt and shame: guilt says, "I
did something bad"; shame says, "I am bad."

Healthy engineering teams build on accountability. A blameless postmortem asks
what allowed a mistake to happen and how to prevent the next one, not who to blame.

## It's not me, it's we

It's easy to treat a career like a solo climb: my code, my review, my promotion.
But when I look at the work I'm proudest of, almost none of it was mine alone.

Thinking in "we" changes how a team works. When wins are shared, people help each
other instead of competing. When problems are shared, the question after
something breaks is "How do we fix it?" instead of "Whose fault is it?"

## Rest is part of the work

THE NUMBER ONE PREDICTOR OF LONGEVITY IS MENTAL HEALTH, NOT PHYSICAL

Rest isn't a reward for finishing the work. It's part of doing the work well.
Tired people miss details, make more mistakes, and have less patience for each
other, and no deadline is worth burning out a whole team.

A sustainable pace isn't a lack of ambition. It's how you stay good at this for
the long haul. (I wrote more about this in
[We Can't Automate Our Way Out of Burnout](/blog/humancapitalintech/).)
